Ask three ad platforms how much revenue your campaigns drove and you will usually get three numbers that add up to more than you actually sold. That is not a bug in any one platform. Each one reports the conversions it can see, using its own rules for what counts.

## What platform ROAS measures
Platform ROAS is the conversion value a platform credits to its own ads, divided by what you spent on that platform. It is useful for comparing campaigns inside one platform, because every campaign there is judged by the same rules.

## Why platform totals overlap
A customer might see an ad on one platform, click an ad on another, and buy a day later. Both platforms can count that sale, because each applies its own attribution rules and neither knows what the other counted. Add the platform numbers together and the same sale is counted twice.

> **Rule of thumb:** never add platform-reported conversions together and call the result a total.

## What blended ROAS measures
Blended ROAS divides the revenue you actually recorded in your own sales system by your total ad spend across every platform. It cannot tell you which campaign worked, but it cannot double count either.

## When to use each

| Your question | The number to use |
| :--- | :--- |
| Which Meta campaign should get more budget? | Platform ROAS, compared within Meta only |
| Is our total ad spend paying off? | Blended ROAS, using your own revenue |
| Did TikTok or Google do better? | Neither alone. Judge each against its objective, then check blended ROAS for the total |

## Report both, and label the source
Show platform-reported figures per platform, clearly labeled as each platform’s own count. Show blended ROAS once, for the whole account, with revenue taken from your own sales system. Then judge each campaign against the objective it was set up for, not against a single blended number.
